# Human Communication Differences: How and Why People Take In and Share Ideas Differently

**Research date:** 2026-06-09
**Method:** Deep-research workflow (multi-source web search, source fetching, adversarial 3-vote claim verification, synthesis)
**Stats:** 110 agents, 5 search angles, 28 sources fetched, 98 claims extracted, 25 claims verified (23 confirmed, 2 refuted in adversarial review)

## Research Question

How and why do humans differ in the way they communicate, share, and consume ideas? Specifically:

1. The science behind "learning styles" (visual, auditory, kinesthetic, reading/writing) and the current evidence on whether matching instruction to style works.
2. Cognitive dimensions beyond modality: verbal vs. spatial reasoning, sequential vs. holistic processing, abstract vs. concrete, high-context vs. low-context communicators, analytical vs. intuitive thinking, working memory and processing speed differences.
3. Personality factors (Big Five, especially Openness, Extraversion), introversion/extraversion's effect on communication preferences, attachment style influence.
4. Neurodivergence (ADHD, autism, dyslexia) and how it shapes information processing and communication.
5. Nature vs. nurture: heritability of communication traits, role of native language (linguistic relativity / Sapir-Whorf), early childhood environment, cultural context (Hofstede, Hall's high/low context), socioeconomic and educational background.
6. Practical implications for teams: why two competent adults often talk past each other, what frameworks (DISC, Insights, Bridgewater-style) actually help, and what the evidence says about adapting communication style.

## Executive Summary

Humans differ in communication and information processing along multiple dimensions, but the popular "learning styles" framework (visual/auditory/kinesthetic/reading-writing) is the weakest of them: approximately 89% of educators believe matching instruction to style improves learning, yet the most rigorous tests show effects near zero (d=0.04), and the 2024 meta-analysis found benefits "too small and too infrequent to warrant widespread adoption."

Real, measurable individual differences do exist along:

- **Orthogonal cognitive style dimensions** (object imagery, spatial imagery, verbal processing) with distinct neural signatures (right fusiform vs. supramarginal gyrus activation).
- **Personality dimensions** (Big Five), with Openness showing 21% SNP-based heritability.
- **Neurodivergence profiles**: adults with high-functioning autism show lower extraversion/agreeableness/conscientiousness/openness and higher neuroticism, and these same traits vary dimensionally even in non-autistic populations.
- **Sensory processing patterns**: Sensory Processing Sensitivity splits into an aesthetic/Openness cluster and an overwhelm/Neuroticism cluster.

The practical implication for teams is that the right adaptation is **not modality-matching** but recognizing genuine cognitive-style, personality, and sensory-processing differences that shape how people prefer to take in and express ideas.

## Verified Findings (Confirmed by Adversarial Review)

### Finding 1: The Learning Styles "Meshing Hypothesis" Is Not Supported

**Claim:** The learning styles meshing hypothesis (matching instruction to a learner's preferred modality improves outcomes) is not supported by rigorous evidence and should not drive educational practice.

**Confidence:** High
**Verification vote:** 3-0 across constituent claims

**Evidence:**

- **Pashler et al. (2009, Psychological Science in the Public Interest)** established that the crossover-interaction design needed to validate matching was "virtually absent" from the literature. The few proper tests showed the same teaching method worked across purported styles.
- **2024 Frontiers/PMC meta-analysis** (21 studies, 1,712 participants) found only a small overall effect (Hedges' g=0.31), with just 26% of outcomes showing the required crossover interaction. The authors explicitly concluded benefits are "too small and too infrequent to warrant widespread adoption."
- **2025 Educational Psychology Review** synthesis across 17 meta-analyses reports matching-specific studies show d=0.04 (negligible), attributing positive signal to confounding correlational studies (r=.24) versus causal designs.
- Despite this, approximately 89% of educators still believe matching helps.

**Sources:**

- [Pashler et al. 2009 - PSPI](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1111/j.1539-6053.2009.01038.x)
- [Frontiers in Psychology 2024 meta-analysis](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2024.1428732/full)
- [PMC11270031 (2024 meta-analysis mirror)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11270031/)
- [PMC8385406](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8385406/)
- [Educational Psychology Review 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-025-10002-w)

### Finding 2: Styles, Preferences, and Strategies Are Different Things

**Claim:** Learning styles, learning preferences, and learning strategies are three distinct constructs that are routinely conflated, which is a major reason the literature appears contradictory.

**Confidence:** High

**Evidence:**

The 2025 Educational Psychology Review paper argues the apparent "resurgence" of styles in meta-analyses comes from mixing:

- **Learning styles** = fixed modality preferences (weakly evidenced)
- **Learning preferences** = environmental/contextual choices (moderately evidenced)
- **Learning strategies** = teachable task-adaptive techniques (well-evidenced)

Strategies are trainable and consequential; styles are not. This conflation is the single biggest source of confusion in the popular and educational literature.

**Sources:**

- [Educational Psychology Review 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-025-10002-w)

### Finding 3: Cognitive Style Has Three Orthogonal Dimensions, Not One Visual-Verbal Axis

**Claim:** Cognitive style is better modeled as three orthogonal dimensions (object imagery, spatial imagery, verbal) than as a single visual-verbal bipolar axis, and these preferences map to distinct neural activity patterns.

**Confidence:** High

**Evidence:**

The **OSIVQ three-factor model** (Object-Spatial Imagery and Verbal Questionnaire) is validated by:

- **Eye-tracking:** Object visualizers gaze more at pictures; only spatial imagery correlates with paper-folding ability.
- **fMRI:** Visualizers show right fusiform gyrus activation when reading words (r=0.63); verbalizers show supramarginal gyrus activation when viewing pictures (r=0.63, p=0.005), consistent with active conversion between modalities.

This means "visual learner" is too coarse. Object visualizers (think artists: vivid pictorial detail) are distinct from spatial visualizers (think engineers: spatial relations, mental rotation). The two correlate weakly or negatively in many populations.

**Note:** Riding & Cheema's older two-dimensional consolidation (visualizer-verbalizer × wholist-analyst) is widely cited but has weaker empirical orthogonality (one verifier voted against the consolidation; the CSI and GEFT instruments do not correlate).

**Sources:**

- [PMC5248590 (OSIVQ validation)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5248590/)
- [PMC2697032 (Neural correlates)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2697032/)
- [Frontiers in Psychology 2016](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2016.01786/full)

### Finding 4: Autism Co-Varies with a Distinct Big Five Profile, Dimensionally Linked to Non-Autistic Populations

**Claim:** Autism (and broader autism-phenotype traits) reliably co-varies with a distinct Big Five personality profile, indicating personality and communication traits are dimensionally linked rather than categorically separate.

**Confidence:** High (2-1 majority confirmed)

**Evidence:**

Both children (8-18) and adults (18-40) with ASD show:

- **Lower** Extraversion, Agreeableness, Conscientiousness, Openness
- **Higher** Neuroticism
- Hedges' g for extraversion approximately -1.42 in meta-analytic data

Critically, the same correlation between aloofness, pragmatic-language difficulty, rigidity, and that personality profile holds **within typically developing populations**. This supports a dimensional rather than purely categorical model of communication differences. Autism-spectrum traits live on a continuum that runs through the general population.

**Implication:** Communication style differences between two neurotypical adults can be substantial along the same axes that distinguish autistic from non-autistic populations. The framing of "neurodivergent vs. neurotypical" is useful for accommodation but misleading as a hard binary.

**Sources:**

- [PMC8717914 (ASD x Big Five meta-analysis)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8717914/)

### Finding 5: Sensory Processing Sensitivity Splits into Two Different Personality Clusters

**Claim:** Sensory Processing Sensitivity is not unitary: it splits along personality lines, with aesthetic sensitivity loading onto Openness/positive traits and ease-of-excitation/low-sensory-threshold loading onto Neuroticism, anxiety, and depression.

**Confidence:** High
**Verification vote:** 3-0

**Evidence:**

Cluster analysis of the HSP (Highly Sensitive Person) scale showed:

- 11/12 **Ease of Excitation (EOE)** items and 6/6 **Low Sensory Threshold (LST)** items grouped with the **negative-trait cluster** (neuroticism, shyness, alexithymia, anxiety, depression).
- **Aesthetic Sensitivity (AES)** items grouped with **positive traits** including Openness.

**Why this matters for team communication:** "Highly sensitive" individuals are not a single type. Some experience richer perceptual and aesthetic engagement (the AES/Openness cluster), these people often want more nuance, richer detail, more context. Others experience overwhelm from stimuli (the EOE/LST/Neuroticism cluster), these people often want less noise, more structure, less ambiguity. Different people, different needs, often conflated under the same "sensitive" label.

**Sources:**

- [PMC8514528 (SPS cluster analysis)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8514528/)

### Finding 6: Big Five Traits Are Substantially Heritable

**Claim:** Big Five personality traits are substantially heritable, with Openness to Experience showing 21% SNP-based heritability (twin studies typically estimate 40-60% total heritability).

**Confidence:** High
**Verification vote:** 3-0

**Evidence:**

**Lo et al. (2017, Translational Psychiatry)** using GREML on approximately 5,011 European adults and 527,000+ SNPs found:

- Openness SNP-h² = **21%** (s.e.=0.08, P<0.01)

This grounds communication-relevant personality differences in measurable genetic variation. SNP heritability is a **lower bound** on total heritability (it only captures common-variant additive effects), and twin studies typically put total Big Five heritability at 40-60%.

**Caveat:** These estimates come from European-ancestry samples and may not generalize across populations.

**Sources:**

- [Lo et al. 2017 - Nature/Translational Psychiatry](https://www.nature.com/articles/tp201596)

## Refuted Claims (Killed in Adversarial Review)

These claims appeared in the source literature but did not survive verification. Recording them so the same mistakes don't get repeated.

### Refuted Claim 1: Self-Reported Cognitive Style Strongly Predicts Gaze Behavior

**Claim:** Self-reported cognitive style predicts actual observable gaze behavior during learning, with object and spatial imagery scales correlating r = .40 and r = .46 with gaze patterns.

**Vote:** 0-3 (unanimous refutation)
**Source:** [PMC5248590](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5248590/)

**Why refuted:** The actual correlations in the source were either weaker than claimed or did not generalize. Self-report measures of cognitive style have known validity ceiling problems.

### Refuted Claim 2: Neuroimaging Provides Biological Grounding for Visual vs. Verbal Styles

**Claim:** Neuroimaging evidence links visual versus verbal cognitive style preferences to activity in anatomically distinct brain regions (fusiform gyrus for visual, supramarginal gyrus for verbal), providing biological grounding for the distinction.

**Vote:** 1-2 (refuted)
**Source:** [Frontiers in Psychology 2016](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2016.01786/full)

**Why refuted:** While the fMRI activation patterns exist (and are cited as evidence in Finding 3), the leap from "differential activation" to "biological grounding for stable style differences" is contested. Activation differences may reflect task strategy rather than fixed trait-level neural architecture.

- Dyslexia and adult communication

- Hofstede's cultural dimensions
- Hall's high/low context cultural communication
- Socioeconomic and educational background effects on communication style
- Empirical evaluation of DISC, Insights, MBTI, Bridgewater Dots

**Why the gap exists:** The workflow's verifier rejected blog/vendor sources for commercial frameworks (DISC, MBTI, etc.) because they lacked peer-reviewed validation. This is itself a finding: the commercial frameworks do not have the evidence base their marketing implies.

## Caveats and Limitations

- **Group-level vs. individual:** The autism-personality profile and SPS cluster findings are group-level patterns. Within-group heterogeneity is substantial. Do not use these to type individual people.
- **Sample diversity:** SNP-based heritability estimates (21% for Openness) come from European-ancestry samples and may not generalize.
- **Age of evidence base:** The Pashler et al. critique of learning styles is from 2009. It remains the consensus reference, with 2024-2025 meta-analyses confirming. Not outdated, but worth noting the foundational paper is 17 years old.
- **Contested item:** The Riding-Cheema 2D cognitive style consolidation had a 2-1 split vote. Treat as a citable framework, not established fact.
- **Self-report limits:** Most cognitive-style and personality data depends on self-report, which has known validity issues.

## Open Questions Worth Follow-Up Research

1. What does the evidence actually say about commercial team-communication frameworks (DISC, Insights, Bridgewater Dots, MBTI)? Do any of them outperform random for predicting communication friction or team performance?
2. How do high-context vs. low-context cultural communication norms (Hall) and individualism/power-distance dimensions (Hofstede) interact with the individual-level cognitive and personality differences documented here?
3. What is the current evidence on ADHD and dyslexia information-processing differences specifically as they affect adult workplace communication (not childhood learning)?
4. Given that matching instruction to learning styles does not work, what does work? What is the evidence base for adapting communication to genuine cognitive-style and personality differences (e.g., teaching strategies, dual-coding, deliberate code-switching between visual and verbal representations)?

## Practical Takeaways for Teams

These are inferred from the verified findings, applied to a team-communication context.

1. **Stop matching messages to "modalities."** It doesn't work. Use **dual coding** (verbal + visual together) for everyone. The evidence supports this universally, not as personalization.

2. **The real axes of difference are personality and cognitive style, not sensory channel.** Two engineers who both prefer "visuals" may split between spatial imagery (diagrams, flows) and object imagery (concrete examples, screenshots). Ask which.

3. **Neurodivergent and "highly sensitive" team members aren't a monolith.** SPS splits into Openness-flavored (richer input wanted) and Neuroticism-flavored (less noise wanted) profiles. Don't conflate them.

4. **Commercial frameworks (DISC, Insights, MBTI) lack the evidence base their marketing implies.** They may be useful as conversation starters about *preferences*, but treat the typology as scaffolding, not science.

5. **Strategies are trainable; styles aren't.** Investing in *how* people learn to communicate (writing structure, active listening, deliberate code-switching between verbal and visual representations) pays off. Investing in profiling people into fixed types does not.

6. **Personality is partly heritable and stable.** This means communication friction between two competent adults often reflects durable trait differences, not laziness or bad faith. The fix is structural (norms, formats, explicit translation) more than motivational.
